== What is this workshop about?

Traditional AI demos show a model answering questions. Production AI requires a complete platform: model serving, governed access, domain grounding, tool integration, observability, evaluation, and security. Each capability solves a real business problem, and they must work together.

This workshop takes you through the full Red Hat OpenShift AI product portfolio by putting you inside a real production deployment. You've just joined *Fed Aura Capital* as an engineer — the AI platform is already running, and your job is to understand every layer of it. Each module introduces a capability, shows you what Fed Aura Capital's setup looks like in practice, and gives you hands-on time with the same tools. By the end, you'll know why the platform looks the way it does and what each component contributes.

== Meet Fed Aura Capital

Fed Aura Capital is one of the largest fictitious banks in the country. Over the last year, the company deployed an AI-powered customer assistant named *Lenora* to support borrowers, loan officers, underwriters, and executives throughout the mortgage lifecycle.

Lenora started as a prototype chatbot connected to an external model. Today, she is a production-grade AI system running entirely on Red Hat OpenShift AI with self-hosted models, domain-grounded knowledge, tool-calling agents, full observability, and defense-in-depth security.

== Your first day: the CTO briefing

As part of your onboarding, your team shares a link to a recorded all-hands from six months ago — the kickoff where your CTO announced the platform rebuild. You hit play.

[quote]
____
When we started, Lenora was a chatbot connected to an external API. Every borrower question left our network, costs scaled with every conversation, and we had no control over what the model knew or how it behaved. Regulators started asking questions. Our CISO blocked production deployment. We had to rethink the whole thing.

We rebuilt the platform in four phases. First, we took control of our own inference — deployed our own models, stopped paying per-token, and exposed them as a managed service. Second, we made the AI actually useful — gave it our policies, our products, our data. Third, we connected it to real tools and made every decision traceable. Fourth, we locked it down: guardrails, adversarial testing, sandboxed agents, full audit trail.

Each of these phases solved a real problem.
____

*Your job*: explore each of those four phases throughout this workshop and understand the platform well enough to support it.

== A quick orientation: try this first

Before diving into the modules, spend a few minutes with Lenora. Log in as Sarah Mitchell (borrower) and try a few of these prompts:

* _"What is the minimum down payment for an FHA loan?"_
* _"Can you estimate how much house I can afford on a $120,000 salary?"_
* _"What documents do I need to submit for pre-qualification?"_

Then log in as David Park (CEO) and ask:

* _"What is our loan approval rate this quarter?"_

Notice what's different. Hold those observations — the next section and the modules ahead will explain exactly why Lenora responds the way she does to each persona.
