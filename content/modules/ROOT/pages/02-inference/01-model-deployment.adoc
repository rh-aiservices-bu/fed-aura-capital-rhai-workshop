== Exercise 1: Explore the Model Catalog

Red Hat AI comes with a curated set of models that are ready for production use. Before deploying anything, you need to know what models are available and how to evaluate them. In this exercise, you will explore the Model Catalog and learn how to identify the right model for your use case.

. Navigate to the *OpenShift AI Dashboard* tab. On the left menu, select *AI Hub > Models*:
+
image::model-catalog-overview.png[Model Catalog showing available models with tags,link=self,window=blank,width=700]

. Look at the tags on the model cards. There are two types of certified models:
+
* *Red Hat AI models* — fully supported by Red Hat with legal indemnification included
* *Validated Models* — third-party models verified by Red Hat to ensure reliable performance and compatibility on OpenShift AI

. Click on any model to open its model card. Notice what information is available:
+
** *Description and tags* — what the model is for, its size, and its architecture
** *License and documentation* — the terms under which you can use and distribute the model
** *Performance insights* — Red Hat benchmarks this model on different hardware across common use cases such as chatbot, code generation, and document summarization, so you can compare expected throughput and latency before committing to a deployment
+
image::model-card-detail.png[Model card showing description, license, and performance benchmarks,link=self,window=blank,width=700]
+
TIP: The performance benchmarks on the model card tell you how the model performs on specific hardware profiles. Use these numbers to size your deployment before you provision GPU capacity.

. Go back to the Catalog and select the *Other Models* tab. This tab shows models that are not Large Language Models, but rather guardian models, embedding models, or the models thar you make available within your organization:
+
image::model-catalog-other-tab.png[Other Models tab showing organization-uploaded models including Qwen3 0.6B,link=self,window=blank,width=700]

. Locate the model called *Qwen3 0.6B*. This is a small open-source LLM — at 0.6 billion parameters, it is compact enough to run on CPU, which makes it a good choice for a quick hands-on deployment without requiring a GPU.

=== What's in the catalog?

Before moving on, take a moment to understand what you just saw:

* The Model Catalog is not just a list of downloads — it is a governed library where each model carries metadata about its origin, license, hardware requirements, and Red Hat validation status
* When Fed Aura Capital's platform team adds a new model, it appears in *Other Models*
